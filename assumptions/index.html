What this draft assumes

This is version six of the rebuilt site. Nothing here is live, and the live site at aidmarketingagency.com is unchanged. Each item below names the decision it waits on.

What changed from V1

  • Ryan reviewed V1 on October 14 and said it was out of alignment, wordy, had no animation, overexplained the method, and showed pricing to the whole world.
  • Every price is gone from the public pages. Pricing is shared on the Speed Check call and on a private checkout link.
  • The method is gone too: no session breakdown, no CRM menu paths, no template text, no scoreboard mechanics. The pages show outcomes.
  • Copy is cut to roughly a third, and it leads with how the result feels.
  • The home page opens with an animated storyboard of a Sunday-night inquiry getting answered, plus two short animated graphics built on sourced numbers.
  • The hero is realigned to one left edge with more room between the words and the picture.

What changed from V2

  • The story now plays even when your computer is set to reduce motion, as gentle fades with Pause and Replay buttons.
  • The page says who it is for at first glance: real estate agents and teams, buyer and seller inquiries.
  • The story card shows one moment at a time instead of five at once.
  • The paper moved from grey-green to a warm cream so the brick red no longer clashes.
  • The question on the real estate teams page about promised results is replaced with how you’ll see the change in your own numbers on day one and day 30.

What changed from V3

  • The story is easier to read. Every beat now holds at least 4.5 seconds, each line of text stays on screen for several seconds, and the fades between beats take 0.6 seconds. The timing is the same with reduce motion on or off. The card flipping to Answered is now its own beat.
  • A fourth feeling is on the Home page: Confident with AI. ChatGPT and Claude stop feeling like homework.
  • A short band on Home and on Real estate teams invites teams to learn the AI tools they keep hearing about, with a plain text row naming ChatGPT, Claude, Follow Up Boss and Lofty. It carries the NAR figure that 63% of agents call the learning curve their biggest tech challenge.
  • The Home page now shows the Harvard Business Review finding next to the response bars: companies that answered an online lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to qualify it as those that waited longer.
  • The pages lead with what you get. The texting question on Done for you now answers Yes, and every negative line on the public pages was rewritten (full list below). Home had none to change.
  • No photo appears on more than one page. The Home “who teaches it” strip uses the phone-call photo, and About Ryan keeps the railing portrait.
  • The draft banner read DRAFT v4 on all nine pages.

  • Real estate teams, FAQ answer about the CRM. Before: “It works with the CRM your team already uses, including Follow Up Boss and Lofty.
