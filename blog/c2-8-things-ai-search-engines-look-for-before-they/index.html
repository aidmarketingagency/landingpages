How to Get Recommended by AI: 8 Things AI Search Engines Look For in Local Businesses

AI search engines are highly selective, recommending only a tiny fraction of local businesses. To get recommended by AI, you need to build broad digital authority through expert-curated lists, consistent review signals, and specialized service pages that AI can easily verify and cite. Basic SEO is no longer enough. The models are looking for proof from third parties, fresh feedback from real customers, and a site structure that makes your services obvious to a machine that has never set foot on a job site.

1. Presence on Trusted Third-Party Lists

AI models do not just look at your website to decide if you are the best roofer or plumber in town. They look at what the rest of the internet says about you. Research on AI overviews in local search (Whitespark, 2025) suggests that the presence of a business on expert-curated lists and similar roundups is a leading factor in AI search visibility.

Think of it like a job reference. If a homeowner asks three neighbors who they would call for a roof replacement and two of them name the same crew, that crew gets the job. AI works the same way. When a reputable industry publication or a local news site lists your shop as a top provider, it acts as a high-confidence signal for the model. It tells the system that your business has been vetted by a third party, not just by your own marketing team.

This is also where a lot of contractors get stuck. They have a decent website and a Google Business Profile, but nobody else on the internet is talking about them. 2. Strong and Recent Review Reputation

Review volume is no longer the only metric that matters. Because consumers are increasingly turning to AI to filter through local options, the quality and freshness of your feedback are critical. BrightLocal research (2026) found that 45 percent of consumers now use AI tools to find local business recommendations. Perhaps more importantly, 88 percent of those users fact-check the reviews cited by AI tools before they ever call you.

Here is what that means in plain English: a pile of five-star reviews from 2022 does not tell an AI model that you are good today. It tells the model you were good three years ago. Fresh reviews are the AI equivalent of a pulse check. If your reviews are stale or negative, the AI is unlikely to include you in its recommendation set.

A common pattern in contractor marketing is that shops pour energy into collecting reviews right after a big push or a slow season, then let the pipeline dry up when things get busy. The shops that win AI recommendations are the ones that keep a steady drip of fresh reviews coming in all year, even during peak season. The model wants to see a business that is actively earning trust right now, not one that earned it once and coasted.

3. High Visibility Across Major Local Platforms

If your business profile is missing or thin on the platforms where people naturally search, you are invisible to the models that aggregate that data. BrightLocal (2025) reports that AI is now the third most-used source for local recommendations, sitting right behind Google and Facebook but ahead of traditional directories like Yelp and TripAdvisor.

To be recommended, your business needs a consistent, active presence on these major platforms. If the data is fragmented or outdated, the AI loses confidence in your business as a reliable recommendation. Think of it like showing up to a job site with mismatched tools. The homeowner sees the inconsistency and starts to wonder if you actually know what you are doing. AI models do the same thing with your business data. They cross-reference what they find on your website against what they find on Google, Facebook, and everywhere else. If the story does not match, confidence drops.

4. Consistent Business Details Across the Web

AI search systems rely on confidence scores to determine if a business is legitimate. If your name, address, phone number, and service area look different on your website than they do on a local directory or a social media profile, the system may struggle to link those data points together.

While precise statistics on this specific signal are hard to isolate, the practical reality is that AI models favor businesses that present a unified, verifiable identity across the entire web. Keeping your NAP (name, address, phone) data consistent is a foundational step in building that trust.

This is the boring stuff that nobody wants to hear about, but it is the stuff that quietly kills your chances. In many contractor shops, the Google Business Profile lists a cell phone number, the website shows an office line, and the local directory still has the old number from two phones ago. When an AI model pulls data from all three sources and gets three different numbers, it does not average them out. It drops the business from the recommendation set because it cannot verify which number is current. Fix the basics first before worrying about anything fancy.

5. Dedicated Pages for Each Specific Service

One of the biggest mistakes contractors make is cramming every service they offer onto a single page. AI search engines prefer clarity. Local SEO research (BrightLocal, 2025) suggests that dedicated pages for each specific service can improve a business's chances of appearing in AI search results.

If you are a roofer, you should have a page for roof repair, a page for roof replacement, and a page for inspection. This structure allows AI to crawl your site and understand exactly what you do, making it much easier for the model to match your site to a specific user query.

Worked Example: How Page Structure Changes AI Citability

Consider two roofing companies in the same market. Company A has one page called "Services" that lists seven offerings: roof repair, roof replacement, gutter installation, attic insulation, skylight installation, emergency tarping, and inspection. Company B has a dedicated page for each service, each with a clear title, service area, response time, and description.

A homeowner asks ChatGPT for "emergency roof tarping near me." The model finds Company B's page titled "Emergency Roof Tarping Service," extracts the service area and response time in a single pass, and cites it directly in the answer. For Company A, the model lands on a single page listing seven services and has to parse through all of them to determine whether tarping is actually offered or just mentioned in a list. That extra parsing step lowers the model's citation confidence. Same services, different structure, completely different AI visibility.

This is an illustration of how structural clarity, not additional marketing spend, changes whether a machine can understand and recommend what you do.

6. Prominence on Industry-Relevant Domains

Not all web mentions are created equal. AI models are trained to prioritize signals from domains that have high authority in your specific trade. Local SEO statistics (BrightLocal, 2025) suggest that prominence on industry-relevant domains can influence how AI ranks local businesses.

This means being mentioned on trade association sites, local chamber of commerce pages, or niche industry directories is likely more valuable than being listed on a generic, low-quality directory that accepts any business with a pulse. A mention on a GAF certified contractor page or a local builder's association directory carries real weight. A listing on a spammy "find any business" site does not.

7. High Citation-Worthiness in AI Answers

When a user asks a question like "who is the best plumber near me," the AI is looking for a business it can confidently cite. Whitespark (2025) found that AI Overviews now appear in 68 percent of local business queries.

If your website is poorly structured, the AI may struggle to extract your service area or your unique value proposition. By optimizing your site for readability and ensuring your contact information is easy to find, you increase your chances of being the specific business the AI mentions in its answer.

Citation-worthiness comes down to making your site scannable. Use clear headings, put your service area in plain text near the top of the page, and make sure your phone number is visible without scrolling. The AI should be able to pull the key facts about your business in one pass, the same way a busy homeowner would.

8. Building Enough Authority to Break into the 1.2% Recommendation Set

The competition for AI recommendations is fierce. The SOCi Local Visibility Index (2026) found that ChatGPT recommends just 1.2 percent of all local business locations in its dataset. This is a sobering number for any shop owner, but it proves that basic listing hygiene is not enough to win.

You need to build a level of digital authority that makes you an obvious choice for the model. This requires a long-term strategy of gathering reviews, earning high-quality mentions, and maintaining a website that is built for clarity rather than just keywords.

The shops breaking into that 1.2 percent are not doing one thing better than everyone else. They are doing eight things consistently, over time, until the AI has enough confidence to put their name in front of a homeowner. It is the same as building a reputation in your local market: trust compounds, but only if you keep adding to it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does AI only recommend a small percentage of local businesses?

AI models prioritize high-confidence data to ensure they provide the most helpful answers. They filter out businesses with inconsistent information, poor reviews, or low web authority. According to the SOCi Local Visibility Index, ChatGPT recommends only 1.2 percent of all local business locations.

How can I get my business featured on expert-curated lists?

Start by building relationships with local media and industry trade groups. Focus on providing unique value or expertise that makes your business a natural fit for these lists, and ensure your business is active in local community events and trade associations.

Do AI search engines care more about review volume or review recency?

Both metrics are important, but recency is a major signal for building trust with AI systems. A business with a large volume of old reviews may appear less relevant than one with a steady stream of fresh, high-quality feedback. AI models prioritize current data to ensure the business is still active and reliable.

Why are dedicated service pages important for AI visibility?

AI models need to map specific user needs to precise solutions. Dedicated service pages allow the AI to crawl your site and understand exactly what you offer. This structure makes it significantly easier for the model to match your business to a specific user query.

How do I make my business easier for AI to cite in search results?

Structure your website so that your business name, address, phone number, and service areas are clearly labeled. Use clear headings, consistent contact information, and specific service pages to ensure the AI can easily extract the information it needs to recommend you.
